1. THE CORE PROBLEM (IN 30 SECONDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under GDPR Art. 17 ("Right to be Forgotten"), clients in a Federated Learning (FL) 
system have the legal right to pull out and demand that their data's influence be 
completely erased from the trained global model.

The real-world issue:
- You CANNOT just delete files, because raw data never touches the server in FL.
- You CANNOT retrain from scratch every time someone leaves. In our 20-round runs, 
  retraining takes over 5 minutes per request. With thousands of edge devices, that's 
  impossible.
- You CANNOT just zero out the output weights (naive masking). Setting bias = -50 
  drops class accuracy to 0%, but the deep convolutional layers still 100% remember 
  the visual features. A simple Membership Inference Attack catches it immediately.

We needed a way to surgically strip the forgotten client's visual influence, keep 
retained accuracy above 90%, and prove it with real visual and privacy metrics.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT WE ACTUALLY BUILT (ARCHITECTURE THAT DIDN'T COLLAPSE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Training vision models in extreme Non-IID federated settings usually fails because 
local batches wreck the model. We fixed this with 3 engineering choices:
1. ResNet-18 with ImageNet Pretraining: Froze layers 1-3, trained only layer4 + head 
   (saved >25% communication overhead).
2. Frozen BatchNorm: Running mean/variance are permanently locked in eval mode. 
   Single-class client batches no longer destroy the normalization statistics.
3. Scaled Cosine Classifier Head: Mapped features and weights to the unit hypersphere 
   with scale = 20. Classification depends strictly on angles, not weight length. 
   Dominant clients with lots of data can no longer overpower minority clients.
4. Centroid Initialization: Initialized class weights to empirical feature centroids 
   at Round 0, giving instant zero-shot class separation before training even began.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE BASELINE EXPERIMENTS: FEDERASER ON CIFAR-100
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We implemented FedEraser (Liu et al., IWQoS 2021) and benchmarked it on an 
enterprise NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU across two setups:

Setting A: Single-Class (1 Client = 1 Class, 10 Clients)
- Client 9 ('bottle') requests unlearning.
- Original FL: 89.1% retain acc, 94.0% bottle acc (40.3s runtime).
- Retrain from Scratch: 89.0% retain acc, 0.0% bottle acc (36.2s runtime).
- FedEraser: 89.7% retain acc, 0.0% bottle acc in 11.7s (3.10x faster than retrain!).
- Where do bottles go? They naturally divert to the closest semantic shapes in 
  feature space: Beds (60%), Beetles (10%), Apples (9%).

Setting B: Multi-Class Non-IID Dirichlet (alpha = 0.5, 10 Clients)
- Client 09 holds 423 private images across multiple classes (177 bottles, 84 beavers, etc.).
- Crucial nuance: Retained clients ALSO hold 323 bottles! Unlearning must NOT erase 
  the concept of a bottle, but purge Client 09's private training memorization.
- At T = 20 rounds (200 total client epochs):
  * Retrained from Scratch took: 300.98s (94.5% test acc, 0.2501 private loss).
  * FedEraser finished in:       80.32s (92.6% test acc, 0.3214 private loss).
  * 3.75x SPEEDUP over full retraining.
  * 95.51% PREDICTION AGREEMENT with the retrained gold standard (404 / 423 samples match!).
  * Private training loss rose from 0.1962 (deeply memorized) to 0.3214 (purged).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW WE AUDITED THE FORGETTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We didn't just look at accuracy; we audited the unlearning in two ways:
1. Grad-CAM Spatial Attribution Drift (|Delta CAM|):
   Hooked into layer4. Measured Pearson correlation drift between attention maps. 
   In Setting A, bottle drift was 0.467 (attention collapsed) while retained classes 
   had only 0.006 drift (zero collateral damage).
2. Membership Inference Attacks (MIA):
   Ran threshold attacks on loss and confidence. 
   In Setting A, MIA-AUC dropped from 0.679 (leaking) down to 0.048 (matching retrain 
   at 0.053). In Setting B, high-confidence member identification at 5% FPR dropped 
   from 8.27% down to 3.07% (a 63% reduction in privacy leakage).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE MAIN ATTRACTION: FED-CNS (OUR NOVEL CONTRIBUTION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why look beyond FedEraser?
Because FedEraser has a massive hidden cost:
- Storage Explosion: It stores round-by-round weights and client updates. At T=20, 
  the cache was 7.2 GB. In a real system with 1,000 edge devices, that's terabytes.
- Retained Client Wake-up: When Client 9 leaves, the server has to wake up the other 
  9 clients to run calibration steps. If clients are offline, unlearning stalls.

Our Novel Idea: Fed-CNS (Contrastive Null-Space via Additive Ledger)
Instead of storing round weights, we track linear sufficient statistics:
- Feature second-moment matrix: A_k = sum phi(x) phi(x)^T  (512x512 matrix = 1.05 MB)
- Class prototype sums:         S_k = sum phi(x)           (10x512 matrix = 20 KB)

Why this is brilliant:
1. Sums are additive: A_all = A_retained + A_forget.
2. When Client 9 leaves, the server simply SUBTRACTS their summary: 
   A_retained = A_all - A_forget (takes 0.1 milliseconds).
3. Retained clients stay 100% OFFLINE! Zero pings, zero battery drain.
4. Server storage drops from 7,200 MB down to 1.07 MB (>2,000x storage savings!).
5. Contrastive Generalized Eigenproblem: The server solves Sigma_f v = lambda Sigma_r v, 
   finds the private directions where Client 9 had high variance, and projects the 
   classifier head into the null space.
6. Execution time: Just 0.515 seconds! (60x faster than retrain, 24x faster than FedEraser).

The Honest Self-Audit (No LARPing):
When we tested Fed-CNS on Setting B, prediction agreement was 96.9%, but the loss on 
Client 9's data was slightly too low (0.2954). Why? 
Because Client 9 held 42% bottles! An unconditioned matrix A_f captured bottle class 
imbalance (label skew) rather than private instance memorization. Also, modifying only 
the head leaves layer4 convolutional weights untouched in a white-box audit.

Our Proposed 2-Tier Fix:
- Class-Conditional Ledger: Store A_{k, c} per class (10 classes * 1 MB = 10.2 MB). 
  This compares Client 9's bottles directly against retained bottles, cancelling out 
  label skew!
- Tier 2 Weight Null-Space: Project unlearning gradients on layer4 into Null(F_retain).
